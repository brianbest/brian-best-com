If you were watching for a big AI product launch the week of June 2-8, you can stop looking. Nothing flagship shipped.

And that's not a gap in coverage — it's the story. I checked OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, Mistral and DeepSeek for anything with a clean GA date inside that window. No model drop, no enterprise SKU, no model card with a date to argue about.

The next morning, June 9, Anthropic dropped Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Same window-adjacent, Google had Gemini 3.5 Live Translate and DiffusionGemma queued for June 9-10. OpenAI had its distribution blitz — Oracle, Ona, a $150M partner network — all starting June 10. But June 2-8 itself? Quiet.

### What actually shipped

One thing did hit a newsroom with a date I can anchor:

**June 2: GitHub Copilot code review for Agent Skills and MCP — in preview.** GitHub flipped the flag to let Copilot review code that uses Agent Skills and Model Context Protocol tooling. It stayed in preview for almost two months after that (it didn't go GA until July 29 for Pro, Pro+, Business and Enterprise). But June 2 is the real start date.

It's easy to miss. It's also exactly the kind of plumbing enterprises live on. If you're building agents that touch your repo, you want review in the same place your engineers already live — not a separate eval harness. This was that plumbing, early.

### What didn't

No ChatGPT Enterprise expansion with a June 2-8 date. No Copilot or Gemini for Workspace bundle change I could verify. No GA from the labs.

And then there's the big one that *started* inside the window but didn't ship inside it:

**June 8: WWDC 2026 opened** (Apple Park + online, through June 12). If Apple Intelligence does anything on-device at scale, that's distribution to a billion pockets — which every enterprise has to plan for. But Apple's newsroom was JS-gated on my pulls, and I couldn't cite a clean product launch with a June 8 date. So I'm not going to pretend I did.

That framing matters. A lot of recaps will tell you GPT-5 or Claude 5 shipped that week. They didn't. I couldn't pull a primary post to back it, and the next verifiable frontier was June 9.

### Why a quiet week matters for enterprise

The enterprise story for early June didn't happen June 2-8. It happened June 9-15. That's when OpenAI put models on Oracle Cloud via Universal Credits, announced the Ona acquisition for persistent Codex execution, and pushed a $150M Partner Network to get to 300,000 certified consultants. That's when BBVA went from 3,000 to 100,000+ ChatGPT Enterprise seats — with 20,000 custom GPTs and a claimed ~3 hours saved per employee per week.

But you can't understand that week without this one.

June 2-8 was the last normal procurement week before AI started being sold where enterprises already buy. Oracle credits. Customer-controlled execution in your own cloud. A channel program instead of a benchmark chart. As that June 14 Partner Network post put it: "The limiting factor for seeing value from AI in the enterprise is no longer model capabilities."

If you're leading adoption, use a quiet week like this one. Don't chase a launch that didn't happen. Re-baseline what you already have in prod — cost per completed task, latency at 128k, how often you trip a safety fallback. The next week's stack assumed you cared about those numbers, not just accuracy.

No new product to buy. But a good week to measure whether you should.
